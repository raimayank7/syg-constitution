How many parts are there?

The original Constitution of 1950 had 22 parts, numbered I to XXII. Since then, Part VII has been repealed (1956) and four parts have been added with a letter after the number: IVA (Fundamental Duties, 1976), XIVA (Tribunals, 1976), IXA (Municipalities, 1992) and IXB (Co-operative Societies, 2011). Part IX itself was replaced in 1992 with the present part on panchayats. Counting the lettered parts and leaving out the repealed one, there are 25 parts in force today.

How articles are numbered

The original Constitution had 395 articles. New articles are not given new numbers at the end. They are inserted where they belong, with a capital letter: Article 21A comes after Article 21, and Articles 243A to 243ZT fill Parts IX, IXA and IXB. Repealed articles leave a gap. So the last article is still Article 395, although the total number of articles in force is now well above 395.

A way to remember the order

The Constitution moves from the country to the citizen to the government: first the territory (I) and its citizens (II), then their rights (III), the goals of the State (IV) and their duties (IVA); then the Union (V), the states (VI), union territories (VIII) and local bodies (IX, IXA); then how the two levels share power and money (XI, XII); then services (XIV), elections (XV), special provisions (XVI), language (XVII), emergencies (XVIII) and amendment (XX).
